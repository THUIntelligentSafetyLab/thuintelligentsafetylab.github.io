Integrated Safety

Integrated safety coordinates active collision avoidance and passive protection to reduce injuries across the pre-crash and in-crash phases. Our research supports this goal through physics-informed risk assessment, multi-fidelity injury prediction, injury-based driving decisions, and human-informed learning with injury-aware evaluation.

Research framework

Objective

Evaluate both whether a collision occurs and how severe its consequences are. We develop complementary prediction, decision-making and evaluation methods, and examine when braking, steering and protective-device activation can reduce injuries.

Risk quantification · AAP 2025

Physics-informed integrated driving risk

8.0% lower IDR MAE vs. GRU1,879 simulator events
Physics-informed training combines neural prediction and vehicle-dynamics guidance (Fig. 4).

Collision likelihood alone cannot describe the potential harm of an emerging conflict. PIRAM combines traffic attention with vehicle-dynamics guidance to estimate collision probability and injury-related severity. IDR MAE was 3.291 versus 3.577 for GRU (8.0% lower) on the simulator split. In one CIDAS reconstruction, the Level III threshold was reached 0.45 s earlier than TTC. Severity uses a collision-speed proxy informed by population injury statistics.

02
Injury prediction · AEI 2026

Knowledge-guided multi-fidelity injury prediction

17.6% lower MAE on average500 HF + 4,000 LF cases
Low-fidelity prior retrieval and high-fidelity residual learning (Fig. 2).

Expensive high-fidelity crash simulations limit the data available for injury prediction. MF-PGRNet combines low-fidelity priors with high-fidelity residual learning. With 500 HF + 4,000 LF cases, MAE was 17.6% lower on average across head, neck and chest criteria than HF-only. On the same 598-case test set, HIC MAE was 78.34 using 500 HF cases, compared with 82.85 for HF-only using 1,000 HF cases, demonstrating more efficient use of simulation labels.

03
Decision optimization · iScience 2022

Human injury-based safety decisions

200 reconstructed crashes55.5% avoided in cooperative S3
Injury prediction guides safety decisions across human, vehicle and traffic levels (Fig. 1).

Safety decisions must account for injury consequences as well as collision avoidance. IRM combines injury prediction with collision dynamics to select braking and steering. Across 200 reconstructed crashes, activation 0.5 s before impact reduced S1 occupant injury severity scores by a median 29.4% versus the original human-driver crashes; cooperative S3 avoided 55.5% of crashes. S3 assumes multiple optimizable automated vehicles, V2V communication and negligible perception/decision delays; these results come from reconstruction simulations.

04
Human-informed learning

Human-informed learning and injury-aware evaluation

~23% lower median per-crash loss14.8% vs. 11.1% collision rate
Driver data collection, trajectory learning and injury-aware evaluation (Fig. 1).

Collision counts alone cannot show how learned driving policies change injury outcomes. Experienced-driver demonstrations train a trajectory model; 5,000 crash simulations support injury-aware evaluation. Versus the CARLA-expert-trained baseline, median modeled loss per remaining crash was 196,669 versus 255,093 (2000 USD), about 23% lower with wide confidence intervals. Collision rate rose to 14.8% from 11.1% in open-loop evaluation, exposing a frequency–consequence trade-off. An explicit injury-minimization training objective remains future work.

Integrated protection for pedestrians

Beyond occupant-focused models and driving decisions, integrated safety also protects vulnerable road users. For pedestrians, braking changes impact conditions and the time available to deploy hoods and airbags, making avoidance and impact protection interdependent. Li et al. (AAP 2025) coordinated braking with pre-triggered hoods and airbags: across 13 hood-impact reconstructions with activation 0.5 s before impact, mean simulated AIS3+ head-injury risk was 27.0% versus 38.4% with braking alone. Sun et al. (AAP 2026) used a 200 ms prediction horizon for active-hood triggering, achieving up to 62.1% lower simulated HIC at 45 km/h than a fixed hood. An offline geometric check found 0 false activations in 110 real near-misses. These findings emphasize injury-aware thresholds and sufficient actuation time; detailed study descriptions remain in the Pedestrian Safety theme.
