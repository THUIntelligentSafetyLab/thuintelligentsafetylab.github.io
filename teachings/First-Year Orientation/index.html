First-Year Orientation Course

This course is designed primarily for first-year undergraduate students and supports their transition from high school to university through general education, scientific inquiry, and personal development. Through small-group seminars and hands-on experiences, students learn about university life and Tsinghua traditions, explore interdisciplinary research through intelligent vehicle cases, and develop self-awareness, resilience, and a sense of responsibility.

Instructor: Bingbing Nie, Hong Wang

Term: Fall

Location: Room 3209, Third Teaching Building

Time: Period 6 on Mondays, Weeks 1–8

Course Overview

This one-credit course consists of 16 hours across eight sessions. It is conducted in a small-class format and combines classroom instruction with experiential learning, interactive discussion, feedback, and student presentations. Classroom and discussion-based activities account for approximately 10 hours, while practical activities account for approximately 6 hours.

The course is organized into three connected modules:

  • Me and My University: Students explore the characteristics of university study and life, learn about Tsinghua’s history, traditions, and culture, and build supportive relationships with teachers and peers.
  • Me and My Major: Using intelligent vehicles as an accessible interdisciplinary case, students examine technological development, experience research and industry practice, and learn the basic process of scientific inquiry.
  • Me and Myself: Students reflect on their interests, strengths, values, and goals, strengthen their resilience and emotional adjustment, and consider the responsibilities that accompany scientific and technological development.

The course includes classroom teaching, activities in natural or social environments, laboratory and industry visits, small-group discussions, practical exercises, and feedback. Exact guest speakers, visit sites, and activity arrangements may vary by year. Current course announcements take precedence.

Prerequisites

  • No prerequisites
  • Primarily intended for first-year undergraduate students

Textbooks and Recommended Reading

  • Yiqi Mei, Mei Yiqi’s Diary at National Southwest Associated University (Chinese-language edition)
  • Guangqian Zhu, On Beauty
  • George Gamow, One Two Three . . . Infinity: Facts and Speculations of Science
  • A Concise History of the Communist Party of China
  • Xiaotong Fei, From the Soil: The Foundations of Chinese Society

Grading

  • Class attendance and discussion: 10%
  • Ongoing course feedback: 20%
  • Practical activity report: 20%
  • Final presentation: 50%

Schedule

Week Date Topic Materials
1 Course Introduction and Icebreaking

An introduction to the course framework and assessment, followed by personal growth stories, small-group icebreaking activities, and a discussion of students’ expectations for university life.
2 Experiencing Tsinghua through the Astronomical Observatory

A visit to the Tsinghua Astronomical Observatory and a stargazing experience, followed by student–teacher discussion on Tsinghua’s history and spirit, the relationship between people and nature, and meaningful long-term goals.
3 Learning from University Peers

Invited upper-year students share experiences of university study, campus life, research, student activities, and personal interests. Speakers and specific topics may vary by year.
4 Intelligent Vehicles and Scientific Research

An introduction to developments in intelligent vehicles, the contributions of different disciplines to emerging technologies, and the fundamental questions, processes, and methods of scientific research.
5 Emerging Technology in Practice

A visit to an intelligent vehicle organization or enterprise, combined with technical exchange and an autonomous mobility experience. The activity connects classroom discussion with real-world technology and industry needs.
6 The Process and Methods of Scientific Inquiry

A laboratory visit, an introduction to research equipment, a case-based account of how a research project develops, and an introductory data-processing exercise followed by reflection.
7 Professional Competence and Well-Rounded Development

Discussion of the development, challenges, and social impact of intelligent vehicles, the professional competence and broader capabilities students can develop at university, and psychological well-being and support resources on campus.
8 Reflection, Interaction, and Student Presentations

Students present their interests, experiences, or personal reflections in a format of their choice, exchange ideas with classmates and instructors, and reflect on the course and their plans for university life.
