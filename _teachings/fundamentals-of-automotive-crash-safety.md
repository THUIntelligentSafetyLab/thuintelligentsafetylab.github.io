## Course Overview

Fundamentals of Automotive Crash Safety (FACS) covers fundamental knowledge, theory, and current technologies in vehicle crash safety and impact protection. It is open to graduate students, international students, and undergraduate students.

Students study vehicle and occupant responses in collisions, injury mechanisms, crash testing and assessment, occupant restraints, pedestrian protection, lightweight vehicle design, and the impact behavior of vehicle structures and materials. The course also addresses adaptive protection for autonomous vehicles and battery failure mechanisms, prediction models, and crash protection for electric vehicles.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Explain the role of vehicle safety in product development and design, including the evolution and limitations of safety technologies.
- Understand crash protection design constraints arising from lightweighting and packaging requirements.
- Explain human injury mechanisms under impact loads and the principles of occupant restraint systems.
- Understand safety risks and protection strategies for autonomous vehicles.
- Understand methods for establishing battery safety envelopes and failure tolerance under impact loads.
- Recognize innovative battery pack designs for electric vehicle crash protection.

## Class Schedule and Teaching Format

The Chinese and English classes share the same course number and teaching team. Students may attend either language class.

The Chinese class meets on Tuesdays from 19:20 to 21:45 (Beijing Time) in Room 3203, Third Teaching Building (3203). Lectures and discussions are in Chinese; slides and supplementary materials are in English. Classes are also broadcast live via Rain Classroom for students who wish to attend online without registering.

The English class combines self-paced study of prerecorded lectures on XuetangX with four in-person sessions during the semester. These sessions may include focused reviews, questions and answers, and, where appropriate, student presentations followed by feedback and discussion. All sessions are conducted in English. The separate English class schedule will be announced after the first teaching week.

The lecture sequence is organized into four modules. Lecture numbers identify course content rather than confirmed teaching weeks. Lecture 5 includes optional crash sensing content, and Lecture 7 is optional.

## Grading

### Chinese Class

Assessment includes three in-class quizzes, four homework assignments, and a final examination, together with class discussion participation and attendance. Homework and examination questions are provided in English; students may write homework, quizzes, and examinations in Chinese or English. Quizzes and the final examination are open book.

### English Class

Assessment includes four homework assignments, a midterm examination, and a final examination, together with classroom discussion participation and attendance. All assignments and examinations are open book and require answers in English.
